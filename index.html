What happens without one

Half your visitors arrive
when you're not working.

People search for a tradesperson at night and at weekends. If the only option is a contact form, most of them close the tab and try someone else.

11pm

The busiest hour for searching

Evenings are when people notice the leak, the broken boiler, the blocked drain. It's also when nobody is at a desk to reply.

Form

A contact form is a dead end

They fill it in, hear nothing for a day, and book whoever answered first. The form captured the lead and then lost it.

90s

Attention doesn't last

A visitor who has to wait for an answer isn't waiting. They're on a competitor's site, and that competitor replied.

On the conversation

What it actually does
when someone types.

Not a canned FAQ widget. A conversation that ends with a job in your diary.

Answers in under a second

No queue, no "we'll get back to you". It replies immediately, at any hour, however many people are on the site at once.

Knows your business, not the internet

Trained on your price list, your services, your hours and your coverage area. It won't invent a service you don't offer or quote a price you'd never charge.

Works out if the job is worth it

Asks what the problem is, how urgent it is and where they are — so the jobs that reach you are the ones worth driving to.

Books the appointment itself

Checks real availability, takes the slot, sends the confirmation and sets the reminder. The job exists before they close the tab.

Captures the ones who leave

If someone wanders off mid-conversation, it has already got their name and number. That's a lead you'd otherwise never have known about.

Hands over rather than guesses

Anything outside what it knows goes to you with the full conversation attached. It never makes something up to fill a silence.
